Look for a real free trial that unlocks payroll — not just a stripped-down free tier — so you can test the part that saves the most time before you pay.

What does "free" really mean for an attendance app?

The word "free" hides two very different things, and knowing which one you are looking at saves a lot of disappointment:

  • A free tier (free forever, but limited): you never pay, but the app caps the number of employees, hides reports, or leaves out payroll. Fine for two or three staff; frustrating the moment you grow.
  • A free trial (everything unlocked, for a set time): you get the full app — including payroll and reports — for a fixed period, then choose a plan. Better for testing whether the app actually saves you time, because the time-saving features are usually the paid ones.

For most small businesses, a generous free trial beats a thin free tier, because the whole point is to see attendance flow into a correct salary figure. If you can only test punching in and out, you have not tested the part that matters.

Five things that separate a good attendance app from a bad one

Before comparing brands, decide what you are actually judging. These five points matter far more than a star rating:

  1. No hardware to buy. If an app needs a fingerprint scanner or a wall-mounted device, you have lost the cost advantage. The best small-business apps run on a phone you already own.
  2. Real proof of presence. A shared PIN or a "tap present" button is easy to fake. Face recognition with a liveness check ties the punch to an actual person standing there.
  3. Attendance becomes salary automatically. This is the biggest time saver. If the app cannot count present days, half-days, week-offs and overtime into a pay figure, you are still doing payroll by hand.
  4. Works across locations from one screen. Even one shop plus a godown is two locations. One dashboard beats separate registers or one machine per site.
  5. Simple enough for your staff. If setup needs a technician or the daily punch confuses workers, it will quietly stop being used within a month.
