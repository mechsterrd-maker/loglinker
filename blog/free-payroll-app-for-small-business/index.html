What does a "free payroll app" actually mean?

"Free" is used loosely, so it pays to read the fine print. In practice you'll meet three models:

  1. Free forever, with limits: the app stays free but caps the number of employees, hides advanced reports, or leaves out statutory deductions. Fine for a tiny team, frustrating as you grow.
  2. Free trial, then paid: you get the full product for a set period — say 15, 30 or 45 days — then move to a monthly plan. This lets you prove it works on real salaries before paying.
  3. "Free" but you pay for extras: the base is free, but essentials like payslip delivery, extra locations or support cost more.

None of these is wrong — but you should know which one you're signing up for. The healthiest choice for most small businesses is a full-featured free trial with transparent pricing afterwards, so there are no surprises once your team depends on it. The features that actually matter for a small team

Payroll software can look intimidating, but a small business only needs a handful of things done well:

  • Attendance that feeds payroll: the single biggest time-saver. If your app records attendance and then calculates salary from it, you skip the copy-paste-into-Excel step entirely.
  • Salary rules set once: monthly CTC, the day-count method (26-day or calendar-day), week-offs and holidays — define them once and let the app apply them every month.
  • Half-days, LOP and overtime: real payroll is full of edge cases. Good software handles loss-of-pay for absences, half-day deductions and overtime without you doing the maths.
  • Statutory deductions: depending on your headcount and state, you may need PF, ESI, professional tax and TDS. Not every free tool includes these — check before you rely on it.
  • Payslips your staff can read: a clear salary slip per person, ideally delivered on WhatsApp so there's no printing and no "how was this calculated?" argument.
  • Reports you can trust: a monthly summary of who was paid what, plus attendance and overtime totals, so you can spot mistakes before money moves.

If a tool nails attendance-to-salary and payslips, it will already save most small businesses a day or two every month. Everything else is a bonus.

A quick word on PF, ESI and professional tax

Once you cross certain headcounts, statutory rules kick in. In simple terms: PF (Provident Fund) generally applies to establishments with 20 or more employees, ESI covers eligible employees below a wage threshold in notified areas, and professional tax is levied by many states on salaried staff. There's also TDS on salaries above the taxable limit. The exact thresholds and rates change, so treat this as a checklist, not legal advice — confirm current rules for your state and size with a qualified professional.

Why does this matter when choosing an app? Because a basic "free" tool may only compute gross pay from attendance, leaving you to handle deductions separately. If compliance matters to you now or soon, pick software that can apply these deductions, or at least export clean data your accountant can use.
