Why look for a biometric machine alternative at all?

Fingerprint attendance machines did a real job for a decade: they stopped the worst of buddy punching and gave you a digital log. But the pain points are consistent, and they are why so many owners now type "biometric attendance alternative" into a search bar:

  • High upfront cost. A decent machine runs roughly ₹15,000–₹40,000 per site, and you pay again for every extra location.
  • Fingers don't always read. Manual workers, cuts, wet or dry hands, and worn fingerprints cause failed reads — so people re-try, queue up, or get marked absent unfairly.
  • It's a shared, touched surface. Everyone presses the same pad at shift start and end. Since the hygiene concerns of recent years, touch-free has become a genuine requirement, not a nice-to-have.
  • Maintenance and downtime. Wall-mounted devices need power, cabling, firmware updates and the occasional service call.
  • It stops at attendance. Most machines just export a log. Turning that into salary is still a manual Excel job at month-end.

What is an app-based attendance alternative?

An app-based system does the same core job — reliably prove who was present, and when — but without any dedicated device. You install an app like LogLinkr on a spare Android phone or tablet, enrol each employee's face once, and mount the phone at the entrance. When a worker arrives, they look at the camera for about a second and they're marked present. The "machine" is a phone you already own.

Because the punch is captured on a connected device, everything syncs to a dashboard you can open from anywhere. There is no register to collect, no device log to export, and no per-site hardware to buy. That single shift — from a fixed scanner to software on a phone — is what makes every advantage below possible.

Is a face-based alternative as secure as a fingerprint?

Yes — and often harder to game. The worry with any attendance method is that one worker marks a friend present. Fingerprint machines reduced this but never fully solved it (cards get shared, and some readers accept a mould or a stored print). A good face system guards against fakery with liveness detection, which checks that a real, present face is in front of the camera rather than a printed photo or a screen. Add the time-and-location stamp on every punch and buddy punching becomes very hard.

On privacy, a well-built app does not keep a folder of employee photographs. It stores a faceprint — an encrypted set of numbers describing the geometry of a face, which cannot be reversed back into a picture — and uses it only to match a punch to the right person. Tell your team what's collected and why, and it's no more intrusive than the ID card most workplaces already use.

How to switch from a machine to the app in one afternoon

You don't need to uninstall anything or call a technician. Run the two side by side for a week if you like, then drop the machine:

  1. Install the app on any spare Android phone or tablet.
  2. Enrol your team by taking two or three photos of each person's face — a few minutes for the whole staff.
  3. Mount the phone at the gate or reception where the machine used to be.
  4. Start punching — employees look at the camera and they're marked present, with the data syncing to your dashboard.

From day one you have a clean digital record and, at month-end, payroll that's already done.
