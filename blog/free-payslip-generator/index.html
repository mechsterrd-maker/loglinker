What is a payslip (and why every business should issue one)

A payslip — also called a salary slip or wage slip — is a simple document that shows how an employee's pay for a period was worked out. It lists what they earned, what was deducted, and the final take-home amount. It sounds like paperwork, but it does three important jobs at once.

For the employee, a payslip is proof of income they need for a loan, a rented home, a credit card or a visa. For you as the owner, it is a clear record that removes the "why is my salary less this month?" argument, because every rupee is shown and explained. And for compliance, issuing wage slips is expected practice under Indian labour and wage rules for most workers, so having one on record protects your business if a dispute ever comes up.

What a valid salary slip must include

Whether you use a free generator, a Word template or an app, a good payslip carries the same building blocks. If any of these are missing, the slip looks unprofessional and may not be accepted as proof of income.

SectionWhat goes in it
HeaderCompany name, address and logo; the words "Salary Slip" or "Payslip" and the month/year it covers.
Employee detailsName, employee ID or code, designation, department, and date of joining if you show it.
Attendance summaryTotal days in the period, days paid, week-offs, leave and loss-of-pay (LOP) days.
EarningsBasic pay, HRA (house rent allowance), other allowances (conveyance, special), and overtime.
DeductionsProvident Fund (PF), ESI, professional tax (PT), TDS if applicable, and any advances or loans.
Net payGross earnings minus total deductions — the take-home amount, shown in both figures and words.
FooterPayment date, mode (bank transfer/cash), and a note that it is a computer-generated slip.

Not every small business deducts PF, ESI or TDS — those depend on your headcount and each employee's pay. If they don't apply to you, simply leave those rows out and show the earnings and net pay. The golden rule is that the numbers on the slip must add up: gross earnings minus deductions must equal the net pay you actually transfer.

How to make a payslip with a free generator (step by step)

Using a free payslip generator is genuinely simple. Here's the flow, whichever tool you pick:

  1. Enter your company details. Add your business name, address and, if you have one, upload a logo. This becomes the header on every slip.
  2. Add the employee. Type the person's name, employee ID, designation and the pay month.
  3. Fill in the attendance. Enter total days, days paid, and any loss-of-pay so the earnings reflect what they actually worked.
  4. List the earnings. Add basic pay, HRA, allowances and overtime. A good tool totals these into gross earnings for you.
  5. List the deductions. Add PF, ESI, professional tax, TDS or advances where they apply. Leave blank the ones that don't.
  6. Check the net pay. The tool subtracts deductions from earnings and shows the take-home figure — confirm it matches what you're paying.
  7. Download and share. Export the slip as a PDF, then print it or send it to the employee on WhatsApp.

Common payslip mistakes to avoid

A few small errors cause most of the month-end disputes we hear about:

  1. Numbers that don't reconcile. If gross minus deductions doesn't equal net pay, staff lose trust immediately. Always let the tool do the arithmetic.
  2. Vague deductions. A single line called "deductions" invites arguments. Break out PF, ESI, PT, advances and LOP separately.
  3. Guessing the days paid. If your payslip doesn't match real attendance, the pay is wrong. This is where linking attendance to payroll pays off.
  4. No record kept. Emailing a one-off PDF and keeping no copy leaves you exposed. Keep every slip on file, ideally in one place.
  5. Skipping months. Issue a slip every pay cycle, not only when someone asks. Consistency is what makes it useful as proof of income.
